        Field Guide

        Understanding Heat Stress

        Learn what heat stress is, what causes it, and why early detection matters.

        What is heat stress?

        Heat stress occurs when a duck's body cannot dissipate heat fast enough and internal temperature rises above safe limits. Early signs are subtle behavioral changes — not just hot air temperatures — which is why thermal imaging is critical for early detection.

        Signs to watch for

        • Open-mouth breathingCooling is no longer sufficient through normal respiratory mechanisms.
        • Wings held away from bodyBirds spread their wings to release more body heat.
        • Reduced activityDucks rest more, eat less, and crowd toward the coolest space.

        Causes of heat stress

        Heat stress is driven by more than temperature alone. Humidity, poor airflow, stocking density, and limited water access all interact to determine whether birds can effectively cool themselves down.

        Main contributors

        • High humidityTraps heat and reduces the effectiveness of evaporative cooling.
        • Poor ventilationCreates hot pockets and stale air where birds struggle most.
        • OvercrowdingRaises collective body heat and leaves fewer cool resting spots.

        Why early detection matters

        Catching heat stress early gives farm operators a window to act before performance drops, welfare is compromised, or mortality risk increases. Use Live behavior cues, Analytics trends, and Insights alerts together for the fastest response.

        What you gain

        • Early warningAct before feed intake and growth begin to decline.
        • Data-driven decisionsMatch thermal readings with barn conditions to prioritize correctly.
        • Better welfareShorten heat events and lower mortality risk across your ducks.

        Support

        Frequently Asked Questions

        Common questions about monitoring, thermal detection, AI guidance, alerts, and duck protection.

        Monitoring How does HeatTrack monitor heat stress in real time?

        HeatTrack combines three sources: a live RGB camera where an AI model detects and counts your ducks, a thermal camera that shows their surface heat, and a DHT22 sensor that measures barn temperature and humidity. Every 30 minutes, a heat-stress model rates the barn conditions and records a Status and Severity Level, so you can act before conditions get worse.

        Thermal What is thermal detection and how does it work?

        Thermal detection uses an infrared camera to read the surface temperature of the ducks in view. The Thermal Monitor shows that heat as an image on a 25–45°C scale, from cooler to hotter. HeatTrack then rates the risk using the four levels of the Thermal Color Spectrum: Green (Low), Yellow (Moderate), Orange (High), and Red (Critical). Infrared can show rising heat before panting or weakness becomes visible, giving you an earlier warning.

        Thermal What do the thermal color spectrum levels mean?

        Colors show relative surface temperature, not an exact clinical body temperature. There are four levels: Green (Level 1, Low Risk) means readings are in the safe range, so keep routine monitoring. Yellow (Level 2, Moderate Risk) means heat is rising, so watch closely and prepare cooling. Orange (Level 3, High Risk) means heat stress is present, so start cooling now. Red (Level 4, Critical Risk) means the ducks are at immediate risk and need emergency action. Green matches the Normal status, Yellow matches Mild, and Orange and Red match Heat Stress. Always check the colors together with the ducks' behavior on the RGB feed.

        AI How do AI recommendations work?

        The Insights page turns your readings into recommendations. Some are based on the current barn temperature, and others on recent Heat Stress records. Each one is labeled with its Severity Level, from Low to Critical, and falls under suggested actions (cooling, moving ducks, hydration), prevention (water, shade, feeding times), environmental steps (airflow, humidity), or temperature follow-ups. The newest recommendations appear first. Tap any card for full details.

        Monitoring What is the difference between Status and Severity Level?

        Status tells you whether a duck is under heat stress: Normal, Mild, or Heat Stress. Severity Level shows how urgent it is, on four levels: Low, Moderate, High, or Critical. Both come from the same reading: Low means Normal, Moderate means Mild, and High or Critical means Heat Stress. Use the status for a quick check and the severity to decide how fast to act.

        Alerts What notifications and alerts does HeatTrack send?

        HeatTrack alerts you when a new record has the Heat Stress status, meaning a High or Critical severity. The alert is added to the Alerts & Notifications panel under the bell icon, and a pop-up appears on screen for a few seconds. Each alert shows the body temperature, severity, and time so you can handle the most urgent cases first. Normal and Mild records do not trigger alerts.

        Alerts Can I control when alerts are delivered?

        Yes. Open the Settings panel from the top bar and switch Alert Notifications on or off. The same panel also turns Camera Streams and the AI Detection Model on or off. Keep alerts on during the hottest hours so you can respond quickly when conditions change.

        Monitoring How often is monitoring data updated?

        The camera feed streams continuously while monitoring is on. DHT22 readings appear as soon as the sensor sends them, updating the Temperature and Humidity tiles and the Environmental Trend chart. Every 30 minutes, a new Status and Severity record is added to the Detection Log History and Analytics charts, and the recommendations update to match.

        Farmers How does HeatTrack help poultry farmers protect their ducks?

        HeatTrack gives poultry and duck farmers earlier visibility into heat risk—combining thermal imaging, environmental sensing, and AI guidance in one dashboard. Teams can intervene sooner, reduce mortality risk during heat spikes, export records for management review, and make data-informed decisions instead of relying on observation alone during the hottest shifts.

        Farmers Should I still check my ducks manually if HeatTrack is running?

        Absolutely. HeatTrack is an assistive monitoring tool—not a replacement for on-site welfare checks or veterinarian guidance. AI detections and recommendations may contain errors. Always confirm alerts with direct observation and follow your farm protocols before taking critical action, especially for High or Critical severity.
