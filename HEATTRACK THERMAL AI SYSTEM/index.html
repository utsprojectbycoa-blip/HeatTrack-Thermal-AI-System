        Support

        Frequently Asked Questions

        Common questions about monitoring, thermal detection, results,
        alerts, and duck protection.

        Getting Started How do I use HeatTrack?

        Check the Home dashboard for the camera feeds, ambient conditions, duck counts, and detection results, and Analytics for the detection history, charts, and Excel reports. Urgent and Critical alerts appear under the bell icon, Settings is under the gear icon, and these questions are under the question mark icon.

        Monitoring How does HeatTrack monitor heat stress in real time?

        HeatTrack combines three sources: a live RGB camera where an AI model detects and counts your ducks, a thermal camera that estimates the surface temperature of each duck's body area, and a DHT22 sensor that measures ambient temperature and humidity. At the first detection and every 30 minutes of monitoring after it, HeatTrack rates the ducks' highest surface temperature together with the ambient temperature and humidity, and records a Status (how heat-stressed are the ducks?) and an Urgency Level (how fast should you act?), so you can act before conditions get worse.

        Thermal What is thermal detection and how does it work?

        Thermal detection uses an infrared camera to read the surface temperature of everything in view. HeatTrack finds each duck and reads the temperature of its body area to estimate its surface temperature. On the Thermal Monitor, a white box shows a duck's estimated surface temperature, and a gray box means that duck could not be measured (at the edge of the frame, too far away, or overlapping another duck). The ducks' lowest, average, and highest surface temperatures are saved with every detection record.

        Thermal Which part of the duck does the surface temperature come from?

        The surface temperature is based on the duck's body area only. It is not taken from the head, eyes, bill, wings, or feet, which can read hotter on the thermal view because heat leaves the body there. The status (Thermal Comfort, Mild, Moderate, or Severe Heat Stress) follows the highest surface temperature among the ducks in each reading, and the lowest and average surface temperatures are saved with it.

        Thermal What do the thermal colors mean?

        The colors show surface heat on a fixed 25–45°C scale, not a risk rating. Black to purple (25–30°C) is the coolest, usually the floor, water, and shade. Red (30–35°C) is warm, such as a duck's feathered body. Orange (35–40°C) is hot, such as the head, eyes, bill, and legs. Yellow to white (40–45°C) is the hottest spot in view. The heat stress rating comes from the Status and Urgency Level, not from the colors. Always check the thermal view together with the ducks' behavior on the RGB feed.

        AI How do the detection results work?

        The Results panel on the Home dashboard shows one result for every 30-minute reading in which ducks were detected, newest first. Each one shows its status, its surface and ambient temperatures, and its Urgency Level, from Routine to Critical. Tap any card for its Detection Details and Summary.

        Monitoring What is the difference between Status and Urgency Level?

        The Status tells you how much heat stress the ducks are under, strictly from their highest surface temperature on the thermal camera: Thermal Comfort (35.7°C or below), Mild Heat Stress (35.8–43.6°C), Moderate Heat Stress (43.7–44.9°C), or Severe Heat Stress (45.0°C or above). The Urgency Level (Routine, Monitor, Urgent, or Critical) tells you how fast to act. It starts at the usual level for the status (Routine, Monitor, Urgent, Critical in the same order), then the ambient temperature and humidity from the DHT22 sensor can move it one level: very hot, humid air raises it and cool air can lower it. So a Mild Heat Stress reading can be Urgent on a hot, humid day. Severe Heat Stress is always Critical. Use the status for a quick check and the urgency level to decide how fast to act.

        Alerts What notifications and alerts does HeatTrack send?

        HeatTrack alerts you when a 30-minute reading has an Urgent or Critical urgency level, whatever its status. The first detection itself does not raise an alert. The alert is added to the Alerts & Notifications panel under the bell icon (orange for Urgent, red for Critical), and a pop-up appears on screen for about 5 seconds. Each alert shows the status, surface temperature, urgency level, and time so you can handle the most urgent cases first. Routine and Monitor readings do not trigger alerts.

        Alerts Can I control when alerts are delivered?

        Yes. Open the Settings panel from the top bar and switch Alert Notifications on or off. The same panel turns Camera Streams on or off. Alerts work only while Camera Streams is on. Turning Camera Streams off also turns off Alert Notifications. When you turn it back on, Alert Notifications comes back the way you left it. Camera Streams is saved for your account, so turning it on or off on one phone or computer applies on every device signed in to it. Alert Notifications is saved in each browser, so every phone or computer you use keeps its own choice. Keep alerts on during the hottest hours so you can respond quickly when conditions change.

        Monitoring How often is monitoring data updated?

        The camera feeds stream continuously while Camera Streams is on. DHT22 readings are saved about every 10 seconds, updating the Temperature and Humidity tiles on Home and the Environmental Trend chart on Analytics. When ducks are first detected, that detection is added to the Detection Log History. After that, a new Status and Urgency Level reading, marked 30 min, is added 20 minutes later (completing the first 30 minutes of monitoring) and then every 30 minutes, and the KPI cards, Analytics charts, and detection results update to match. Periods with no ducks detected, or with ducks the thermal camera could not measure, add no record, and if there is no reading for 10 minutes (no ducks, or ducks without a surface temperature), the next detection starts a new count.

        Farmers How does HeatTrack help poultry farmers protect their ducks?

        HeatTrack gives poultry and duck farmers earlier visibility into heat risk—combining thermal detection, environmental sensing, and urgency alerts in one dashboard. Teams can intervene sooner, reduce mortality risk during heat spikes, export records for management review, and make data-informed decisions instead of relying on observation alone during the hottest shifts.

        Farmers Should I still check my ducks manually if HeatTrack is running?

        Absolutely. HeatTrack is an assistive monitoring tool—not a replacement for on-site welfare checks or veterinarian guidance. AI detections may contain errors. Always confirm alerts with direct observation and follow your farm protocols before taking critical action, especially for Urgent or Critical urgency levels.
