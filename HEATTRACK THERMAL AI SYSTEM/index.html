        Quick Reference

        Understand Every Reading

        What the thermal colors mean, and how HeatTrack rates heat stress in your ducks.

        Thermal Color Spectrum

        The Thermal Monitor on the Live page shows surface heat on a fixed 25–45°C color scale: dark purple is coolest and pale yellow is hottest. Each duck the system finds gets a box. A white box shows the duck's estimated surface temperature, taken from its hottest spot (marked with a white cross). A gray box means the duck could not be measured, for example when it is at the edge of the frame, too far away, or overlapping another duck. The colors show heat only; the heat stress rating comes from the Status & Urgency levels below.

        25–30°C 30–35°C 35–40°C 40–45°C
        Cooler Hotter
        1. 25–30°C
          Black to Purple — Coolest

          The coolest surfaces in view, usually the background: floor, water, and shaded areas. Anything at or below 25°C shows as black.

          Cool
        2. 30–35°C
          Red — Warm

          Warm surfaces, such as a duck's feathered body, where feathers hold most of the heat in, or areas warmed by the sun.

          Warm
        3. 35–40°C
          Orange — Hot

          Hot spots where heat leaves the body, such as the head, eyes, bill, and legs. These spots are not used for the rating: HeatTrack takes each duck's surface temperature from its body area.

          Hot
        4. 40–45°C
          Yellow to White — Hottest

          The hottest spots in view. Anything at or above 45°C shows as the palest color. Check the box labels for each duck's estimated surface temperature.

          Very Hot

        Status & Urgency Levels

        At the first detection and every 30 minutes of monitoring after it, HeatTrack records a reading with two labels. The Status tells you how much heat stress the ducks are under: Thermal Comfort, Mild Heat Stress, Moderate Heat Stress, or Severe Heat Stress. The Urgency Level (Routine, Monitor, Urgent, or Critical) tells you how fast to act. The status follows the ducks' highest surface temperature measured by the thermal camera in that period (the body only, not the head, eyes, wings, or feet), strictly by the temperature ranges shown below. The urgency starts at the usual level for that status, then the ambient temperature and humidity from the DHT22 sensor can move it one level: very hot, humid air raises it, and cool air can lower it. So a Mild Heat Stress reading can be Urgent on a hot, humid day. Severe Heat Stress is always Critical. Each record also lists the ducks' lowest and average surface temperatures and the ambient temperature and humidity. Check the status at a glance, then let the urgency level tell you how fast to act.

        Getting Started

        How to Use HeatTrack

        A quick guide to monitoring your ducks, from setting up your device to reviewing reports.

        1. Device

          Set Up Your Device

          Place the HeatTrack device so its RGB and thermal cameras face the ducks, with the DHT22 sensor inside the pen. Plug it in and connect it to the internet. It starts monitoring on its own every time it turns on.

        2. Sign Up

          Link It to Your Account

          Sign up with your details and your device's Device ID (HT-PDCK- plus the 3-digit number) and API Key, so its readings go to your account. You can log in once the administrator approves it.

        3. Monitor

          Watch the Live Page

          Open Live to see the camera feeds, duck count, and ambient temperature and humidity. ACTIVE on the sensor panel means your device is connected. Every 30 minutes, ducks are rated Thermal Comfort, Mild Heat Stress, Moderate Heat Stress, or Severe Heat Stress.

        4. Insights

          Follow the Insights

          Open Insights for duck counts and AI recommendations. Click any result to see its details and the suggested actions. Each result shows its status, from Thermal Comfort to Severe Heat Stress, and an urgency level from Routine to Critical, so you know what to act on first.

        5. Analytics

          Review Analytics

          Open Analytics to browse the Detection Log History, filter by status or date, view the charts, and click Generate Report to download an Excel file.

        6. Alerts

          Manage Alerts & Settings

          The bell icon lists your Urgent and Critical alerts. The gear icon opens Settings, where you can turn the cameras and alert notifications on or off.

        Field Guide

        Understanding Heat Stress

        Learn what heat stress is, what causes it, and why early detection matters.

        What is heat stress?

        Heat stress occurs when a duck cannot shed heat fast enough and its surface temperature rises above safe limits. HeatTrack rates each reading's status from the ducks' highest surface temperature, read by a thermal camera, and its urgency level from that status together with the ambient temperature and humidity.

        Signs to watch for

        • Open-mouth breathingCooling is no longer sufficient through normal respiratory mechanisms.
        • Wings held away from bodyBirds spread their wings to release more body heat.
        • Reduced activityDucks rest more, eat less, and crowd toward the coolest space.

        Causes of heat stress

        Heat stress is driven by more than temperature alone. Humidity, poor airflow, stocking density, and limited water access all interact to determine whether birds can effectively cool themselves down.

        Main contributors

        • High humidityTraps heat and reduces the effectiveness of evaporative cooling.
        • Poor ventilationCreates hot pockets and stale air where birds struggle most.
        • OvercrowdingRaises collective body heat and leaves fewer cool resting spots.

        Why early detection matters

        Catching heat stress early gives farm operators a window to act before performance drops, welfare is compromised, or mortality risk increases. Use the Live feeds, Analytics records, alerts, and Insights together.

        What you gain

        • Early warningAct before feed intake and growth begin to decline.
        • Data-driven decisionsMatch thermal readings with ambient conditions to prioritize correctly.
        • Better welfareShorten heat events and lower mortality risk across your ducks.

        Support

        Frequently Asked Questions

        Common questions about monitoring, thermal detection, AI guidance,
        alerts, and duck protection.

        Getting Started How do I use HeatTrack?

        Check the Live page for the camera feeds and ambient conditions, Insights for duck counts and AI recommendations, and Analytics for the detection history, charts, and Excel reports. Urgent and Critical alerts appear under the bell icon, and Settings is under the gear icon. For the full step-by-step guide, see How to Use HeatTrack on the About page.

        Monitoring How does HeatTrack monitor heat stress in real time?

        HeatTrack combines three sources: a live RGB camera where an AI model detects and counts your ducks, a thermal camera that estimates the surface temperature of each duck's body area, and a DHT22 sensor that measures ambient temperature and humidity. At the first detection and every 30 minutes of monitoring after it, HeatTrack rates the ducks' highest surface temperature together with the ambient temperature and humidity, and records a Status (how heat-stressed are the ducks?) and an Urgency Level (how fast should you act?), so you can act before conditions get worse.

        Thermal What is thermal detection and how does it work?

        Thermal detection uses an infrared camera to read the surface temperature of everything in view. HeatTrack finds each duck and reads the temperature of its body area to estimate its surface temperature. On the Thermal Monitor, a white box shows a duck's estimated surface temperature, and a gray box means that duck could not be measured (at the edge of the frame, too far away, or overlapping another duck). The ducks' lowest, average, and highest surface temperatures are saved with every detection record.

        Thermal Which part of the duck does the surface temperature come from?

        The surface temperature is based on the duck's body area only. It is not taken from the head, eyes, bill, wings, or feet, which can read hotter on the thermal view because heat leaves the body there. The status (Thermal Comfort, Mild, Moderate, or Severe Heat Stress) follows the highest surface temperature among the ducks in each reading, and the lowest and average surface temperatures are saved with it.

        Thermal What do the thermal colors mean?

        The colors show surface heat on a fixed 25–45°C scale, not a risk rating. Black to purple (25–30°C) is the coolest, usually the floor, water, and shade. Red (30–35°C) is warm, such as a duck's feathered body. Orange (35–40°C) is hot, such as the head, eyes, bill, and legs. Yellow to white (40–45°C) is the hottest spot in view. The heat stress rating comes from the Status and Urgency Level, not from the colors. Always check the thermal view together with the ducks' behavior on the RGB feed.

        AI How do AI recommendations work?

        The Insights page shows one recommendation for every 30-minute reading in which ducks were detected, newest first. Each one is labeled with its Urgency Level, from Routine to Critical, and its steps are matched to that level. When available, AI rewrites them for the current conditions, marked with an AI badge. Tap any card for its Detection Details, Summary, Suggested Actions (cooling, moving ducks, hydration), and Heat Stress Prevention (airflow, shade, water, feeding times).

        Monitoring What is the difference between Status and Urgency Level?

        The Status tells you how much heat stress the ducks are under, strictly from their highest surface temperature on the thermal camera: Thermal Comfort (35.7°C or below), Mild Heat Stress (35.8–43.6°C), Moderate Heat Stress (43.7–44.9°C), or Severe Heat Stress (45.0°C or above). The Urgency Level (Routine, Monitor, Urgent, or Critical) tells you how fast to act. It starts at the usual level for the status (Routine, Monitor, Urgent, Critical in the same order), then the ambient temperature and humidity from the DHT22 sensor can move it one level: very hot, humid air raises it and cool air can lower it. So a Mild Heat Stress reading can be Urgent on a hot, humid day. Severe Heat Stress is always Critical. Use the status for a quick check and the urgency level to decide how fast to act.

        Alerts What notifications and alerts does HeatTrack send?

        HeatTrack alerts you when a 30-minute reading has an Urgent or Critical urgency level, whatever its status. The first detection itself does not raise an alert. The alert is added to the Alerts & Notifications panel under the bell icon (orange for Urgent, red for Critical), and a pop-up appears on screen for about 5 seconds. Each alert shows the status, surface temperature, urgency level, and time so you can handle the most urgent cases first. Routine and Monitor readings do not trigger alerts.

        Alerts Can I control when alerts are delivered?

        Yes. Open the Settings panel from the top bar and switch Alert Notifications on or off. The same panel turns Camera Streams on or off. Alerts work only while Camera Streams is on. Turning Camera Streams off also turns off Alert Notifications. When you turn it back on, Alert Notifications comes back the way you left it. Camera Streams is saved for your account, so turning it on or off on one phone or computer applies on every device signed in to it. Alert Notifications is saved in each browser, so every phone or computer you use keeps its own choice. Keep alerts on during the hottest hours so you can respond quickly when conditions change.

        Monitoring How often is monitoring data updated?

        The camera feeds stream continuously while Camera Streams is on. DHT22 readings are saved about every 10 seconds, updating the Temperature and Humidity tiles and the Environmental Trend chart. When ducks are first detected, that detection is added to the Detection Log History. After that, a new Status and Urgency Level reading, marked 30 min, is added 20 minutes later (completing the first 30 minutes of monitoring) and then every 30 minutes, and the KPI cards, Analytics charts, and recommendations update to match. Periods with no ducks detected, or with ducks the thermal camera could not measure, add no record, and if there is no reading for 10 minutes (no ducks, or ducks without a surface temperature), the next detection starts a new count.

        Farmers How does HeatTrack help poultry farmers protect their ducks?

        HeatTrack gives poultry and duck farmers earlier visibility into heat risk—combining thermal detection, environmental sensing, and AI guidance in one dashboard. Teams can intervene sooner, reduce mortality risk during heat spikes, export records for management review, and make data-informed decisions instead of relying on observation alone during the hottest shifts.

        Farmers Should I still check my ducks manually if HeatTrack is running?

        Absolutely. HeatTrack is an assistive monitoring tool—not a replacement for on-site welfare checks or veterinarian guidance. AI detections and recommendations may contain errors. Always confirm alerts with direct observation and follow your farm protocols before taking critical action, especially for Urgent or Critical urgency levels.
